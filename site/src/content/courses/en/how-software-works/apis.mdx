## Introduction

You'll hear "API" in almost every conversation about software. It sounds technical, but the idea comes from everyday life. By the end of this lesson your page will show live data from one.

## Lesson overview

This section contains a general overview of topics that you will learn in this lesson.

- What an API is.
- What requests, responses, endpoints and API docs are.
- Where APIs show up in apps you already use.
- How to spot fake data.

## A waiter between programs

Step through the picture:

<ApiWaiter />

**An API is a waiter between programs.** Your app doesn't walk into another company's kitchen. It orders from a menu, the waiter takes the order in, and the dish comes back out.

| Word | In the restaurant | In software |
|---|---|---|
| **API** | the waiter | the way one program asks another for something |
| **API docs** | the menu | the page that lists what you can ask for, and how |
| **Request** | your order | what your app asks for |
| **Response** | the dish | what comes back (data, or an error) |
| **Endpoint** | one item on the menu | one specific thing you can ask for |

API stands for *Application Programming Interface*. Nobody uses the long name.

## APIs in the wild

APIs are behind most of what your phone does. A weather widget asks a **weather API**. "Log in with Google" asks **Google's login API** whether you're really you. A delivery app shows a map from a **maps API** and charges your card through a **payments API**. An app with an AI assistant sends your message to an **AI API**.
