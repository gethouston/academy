## Introduction

It's the most common first message to an AI agent: one sentence holding a whole dream. *"GTA 6 but multiplayer." "An app that runs my whole company." "Airbnb for dogs, with payments and chat."* The agent will try. It might even produce something. But it's almost never what you wanted, and when it breaks, nobody can tell why.

## Lesson overview

This section contains a general overview of topics that you will learn in this lesson.

- Why giant requests fail, for anyone.
- What a slice is.
- How to write slices as things a person can do.
- What to do when an agent builds too much.

## Why giant requests fail

It isn't that the AI is dumb. Giant requests fail for **anyone**, including a team of senior engineers:

- **You can't test it.** If a thousand things changed, you can't check them all.
- **You can't tell what broke.** The cause hides among a thousand pieces.
- **The agent has to guess.** A one-line dream leaves hundreds of decisions open, and wrong guesses pile up.
- **You stop understanding your own product.** If you didn't watch it grow, you can't steer it.

## A slice

A **slice** is a small piece of your product that works from start to finish (a person can actually use it, even if it does very little), is small enough to build in one go and check in about five minutes, and has a **"done when"**: a sentence anyone can check without reading code.

| Not a slice | A slice |
|---|---|
| "The multiplayer system" | "A second player appears on the same street" |
| "The backend" | "A request I type in is still there after I refresh" |
| "Make the app nice" | "The request form fits on a phone screen, without zooming" |
| "Payments, chat and reviews" | "A dog owner sees a list of three sitters (fake data is fine)" |

Slices are written as **what a person can do**, not as technical parts. That's how you'll test them.

<RedFlag title="The agent builds 15 features in one go">
	You asked for slice 1 and got a dashboard, settings, login, notifications and a dark mode, and half of them don't work. Don't try to fix it all. Say: *"Let's go back to just slice 1. Remove everything else, and tell me how to check that slice 1 works."*
</RedFlag>

## Assignment

1. Write your idea in one sentence, as big as it is. No idea yet? Use one of these: *a booking page for a small business*, *a client portal where customers see the status of their order*, *a request tracker for your team*.
2. In a new Claude session, send:

   <PromptCard>
   Here's my idea: [your idea, in one sentence]. Don't build anything yet. Help me slice it. First ask me up to 5 questions about who it's for and what problem it solves. Then propose: the smallest useful version, in one sentence, and 3 to 6 slices in order, each one something a person can do, with a "done when" I can check myself in five minutes, without reading code.
   </PromptCard>

3. If you installed the tutor, `/academy:brief` does the same and saves the result as a one-page brief in your project.
